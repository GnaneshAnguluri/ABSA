Aspect Sentiment Triplet Extraction (ASTE): End-to-End Experimental Report

1. Executive Summary

This report documents the architectural evolution and experimental milestones of our Aspect Sentiment Triplet Extraction (ASTE) system. ASTE requires the joint extraction of: 1. Aspect terms (e.g., "battery life", "pizza crust") 2. Opinion terms (e.g., "terrible", "exceptionally crispy") 3. Sentiment polarities (Positive, Negative, Neutral)

Strict exact-match evaluation requires that all three components match ground-truth boundaries and labels simultaneously: $$\text{Triplet} = \big((\text{asp_start}, \text{asp_end}), (\text{opn_start}, \text{opn_end}), \text{polarity}\big)$$

Our experimental journey progressed through three primary stages: 1. Phase 1: Encoder Selection & Exploration across Transformer backbones. 2. Phase 2: Full Benchmark Corpus Fine-Tuning scaling cross-domain review adaptation with linear heads. 3. Phase 3: Classifier Architecture Evolution replacing naive linear projections with a 3,072-dimensional Non-Linear Interaction Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) architecture.

2. Phase 1: Encoder Exploration & Baseline Establishment

2.1 Evaluated Encoder Architectures

We systematically benchmarked three major pretrained transformer backbones to identify the optimal contextual sequence encoder for fine-grained Aspect Sentiment Triplet Extraction: - bert-base-uncased (Google): 12-layer, 768-dim bidirectional encoder trained on BooksCorpus and English Wikipedia with WordPiece tokenization. - activebus/BERT-XD_Review: Domain-adapted BERT further pretrained on large-scale e-commerce review corpora (Amazon and Yelp). - FacebookAI/roberta-base: Optimized BERT variant trained on larger batches and 160GB of uncompressed text with byte-level Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE).

2.2 Phase 1 Baseline Results (14res Benchmark)

All backbones were evaluated using identical classification heads under the frozen representation paradigm on the SemEval 2014 Restaurant (14res) benchmark:

2.3 Rationale for Selecting bert-base-uncased

Although BERT-XD_Review exhibited slightly higher precision on the single restaurant domain, bert-base-uncased was rigorously chosen as our definitive project backbone based on three decisive factors:

  1. Superior Overall Generalization & Balanced Metrics:
  2. Standard bert-base-uncased achieved the highest test strict F1 score (38.95% vs. 38.80% for roberta-base), demonstrating superior balance between precision (37.65%) and recall (40.34%).

  3. In contrast, BERT-XD suffered from an over-conservative recall penalty (37.63%), while roberta-base generated excessive false positives (precision dropping to 35.04%).

  4. Subword Tokenization & Strict Span Boundary Alignment:

  5. ASTE evaluates exact span boundaries: missing or including a single leading/trailing subword or punctuation marks an entire triplet as $0$.

  6. BERT’s WordPiece tokenizer preserves strict lexical boundaries with explicit ## subword continuations. Conversely, RoBERTa’s byte-level BPE treats leading whitespaces as separate byte tokens (Ġ), introducing subtle span-alignment discrepancies when mapping predicted token indices back to original character/word offsets.

  7. Cross-Domain Neutrality (Preventing Negative Transfer):

  8. Because our downstream plan involved expanding to heterogeneous domains—specifically laptop technical hardware reviews (14lap) alongside restaurant reviews (14res, 15res, 16res)—domain-specialized models like BERT-XD pose a known risk of negative transfer due to over-indexing on restaurant-specific colloquialisms.

  9. Standard bert-base-uncased provided the most robust, domain-neutral representation for subsequent multi-dataset scaling.

  10. Literature Standardization:

  11. Seminal ASTE architectures (Peng et al., 2020; Xu et al., 2020; GTS, 2021) establish bert-base-uncased as the canonical benchmark architecture, ensuring complete experimental validity and direct comparability with published state-of-the-art results.

3. Phase 2: Full Benchmark Corpus Fine-Tuning

3.1 Motivation & Methodology

In Phase 1, the encoder was frozen, forcing classification heads to operate on static contextual representations. In Phase 2, we un-froze all 12 Transformer layers (requires_grad=True) and optimized the model across the combined SemEval ASTE benchmark suites: - SemEval 2014 Restaurant (14res) - SemEval 2014 Laptop (14lap) - SemEval 2015 Restaurant (15res) - SemEval 2016 Restaurant (16res)

We employed differential optimization: - BERT Encoder Learning Rate: $1 \times 10^{-5}$ (with AdamW weight decay $0.01$) - ASTE Classification Heads Learning Rate: $5 \times 10^{-4}$ - Batch Size: 8 sentences per batch

3.2 Phase 2 Benchmark Results

The multi-dataset corpus adaptation produced substantial gains over the Phase 1 frozen baseline across all 4 benchmarks:

Phase 2 Analysis: By unfreezing BERT representations across all domains, overall strict triplet extraction surged from ~38% to 71.09% F1, capturing 1,868 out of 2,536 total gold triplets. However, error analysis revealed that standard linear projections (Linear(1536, 2)) limited further precision gains.

4. Phase 3: Classifier Architecture Evolution (Non-Linear Interaction MLPs)

4.1 Architectural Limitation of Phase 2

In Phase 2, candidate pairs were formed by simple vector concatenation: $$\mathbf{h}{\text{pair}} = [\mathbf{h}{\text{asp}} \,|\, \mathbf{h}_{\text{opn}}] \in \mathbb{R}^{1536}$$ This linear representation forced the relation classifier to model span interactions via a single matrix multiplication without explicit similarity or difference metrics.

4.2 Upgraded Model Architecture (ASTEModelV2)

To overcome this limitation, we engineered ASTEModelV2 with three architectural enhancements:

  1. 2-Layer MLP Sequence Heads: Replaced single-layer BIO classifiers with 2-layer MLPs featuring Layer Normalization, GELU activations, and Dropout: $$\text{Logits}_{\text{BIO}} = \mathbf{W}_2 \cdot \text{GELU}(\text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{W}_1 \mathbf{h}_t))$$
  2. 3,072-Dimensional Pair Feature Engineering: Constructed rich non-linear interaction features combining concatenation, absolute element-wise distance, and the Hadamard product: $$\mathbf{h}{\text{pair}} = \big[\,\mathbf{h}{\text{asp}} \;\big|\; \mathbf{h}{\text{opn}} \;\big|\; |\mathbf{h}{\text{asp}} - \mathbf{h}{\text{opn}}| \;\big|\; (\mathbf{h}{\text{asp}} \odot \mathbf{h}_{\text{opn}})\,\big] \in \mathbb{R}^{3072}$$
  3. Deep Non-Linear Relation & Sentiment Classifiers: $$\text{PairScore} = \text{Linear}{512 \to C}\Big(\text{Dropout}\big(\text{GELU}(\text{LayerNorm}(\text{Linear}{3072 \to 512}(\mathbf{h}_{\text{pair}})))\big)\Big)$$

Key Conclusions:

  1. Classifier Depth Matters: Moving from single linear projections to 2-layer MLPs with interaction vectors ($|\mathbf{h}_a - \mathbf{h}_o|$ and $\mathbf{h}_a \odot \mathbf{h}_o$) broke through the linear ceiling on 14res, jumping from 66.37% to 74.38% F1.
  2. Consistent Performance: All four benchmark datasets converged to a balanced 73% – 74% F1 range with strong recall ($\ge 71\%$).
  3. High Absolute Recall: The final system extracts 1,956 out of 2,536 exact triplets under strict boundaries across both restaurant and laptop domains.
